Milo House

米洛農場住宅  ·  Elizabeth Herrmann Architecture + Design  ·  Cornwall, Vermont, USA
Residential Architecture – Rural Retreat · Dezeen Featured July 2026
Design Reading
Milo House is a 195-square-metre residence designed by Elizabeth Herrmann Architecture + Design on a former dairy farm in Cornwall, Vermont, featured on Dezeen in July 2026. The programme is anchored by a double-height great room whose floor-to-ceiling glazing frames the pastoral landscape in its entirety; a metal gable roof distinguishes the living volume from the single-storey sleeping wings that extend across the former farmstead. Raw pine panelling, concrete masonry, and blackened-steel hardware constitute a three-part material palette calibrated for Vermont's severe winters and generous summers alike. The spatial sequence unfolds from a compressed entry hall through a stepped transition into the full-panorama kitchen-dining zone before arriving at the quieter bedroom wing oriented toward the apple orchard, using the farm's daily rhythms as the underlying logic of the circulation. The project was recognised by Dezeen for the precision with which it balances regional vernacular with contemporary spatial economy.

Casa Mac Annexe

麥克宅廂房  ·  CTA  ·  Phú Mỹ, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
Residential Architecture – Tropical Annexe · Dezeen Featured July 2026
Design Reading
CTA added a 120-square-metre annexe to an existing villa in Phú Mỹ, Ho Chi Minh City, connecting to the main house via a covered breezeway. The new structure is built entirely from load-bearing brick with a mono-pitch terracotta tile roof—materials chosen for their thermal mass in the tropical climate: 30-centimetre masonry walls absorb heat during the day and release it slowly at night, eliminating mechanical cooling under normal conditions. A north-facing garden wall punctuated by vertical slit openings creates a filtered light environment that maintains visual privacy without sacrificing cross-ventilation. The annexe sustains the main villa's brick vernacular while its contemporary mono-pitch profile and exposed masonry texture establish a legible identity as a distinct addition. Dezeen featured the project on 27 July 2026 as an exemplar of the contemporary reassessment of vernacular building techniques in Southeast Asian residential work.

AJ Small Projects 2026

Castlands Road

卡斯特蘭斯路自建宅  ·  Bricolage  ·  Lewisham, London, UK
Overall Winner – AJ Small Projects Award 2026
Design Reading
Bricolage designed and built this self-commissioned Lewisham terraced house on a budget of £280,000, winning the AJ Small Projects Award 2026 Overall Prize and reported by Dezeen on 29 July. The project maximises a 5.5-metre party-wall plot through a strategic section: living spaces step down to a partially buried garden level, gaining 3.2-metre ceiling height from natural grade changes without triggering planning height restrictions. Cross-laminated timber panels form the structural shell; lime render and reclaimed brick complete the exterior finish while the CLT is left exposed internally, paired with terrazzo floors and handcrafted pendant lights. The architect led the design, procurement, and construction throughout, dissolving the conventional client-designer-contractor triangle. Judges praised the project for demonstrating that architect-led self-build and rigorous budget discipline are not mutually exclusive, making it the highest small-scale architectural honour in the UK for 2026.

Darlinghurst House

達令赫斯特歷史排屋  ·  Anthony Gill Architects  ·  Darlinghurst, Sydney, NSW, Australia
Hugh and Eva Buhrich Award – Houses (Alterations & Additions) · AIA NSW 2026
Design Reading
Anthony Gill Architects transformed an intact Victorian terrace in inner-Sydney Darlinghurst through a series of surgical interventions that preserve the existing fabric while reordering the domestic sequence. A new rear wing of concrete and reclaimed brick opens to a planted light well, drawing sky into the previously compressed kitchen-dining zone and severing direct sightlines to neighbouring party walls. The architect maintained all original plasterwork ceiling roses, dado rails, cornices, and joinery—preserving the complete Victorian interior—while threading contemporary insertions between the existing ornament: polished concrete pavers, handcrafted steel-framed windows, ceramic tiles. The approach uses material texture rather than contrast as its operative principle. The spatial sequence moves from a compressed heritage street hall through a corridor of Victorian rooms to the sky-lit contemporary kitchen core, enacting a gradual transition from historical density to contemporary openness. The project received the 2026 Hugh and Eva Buhrich Award—AIA NSW's premier honour for residential alterations and additions. Photography by Rory Gardiner.

Anglesea Beach House

安格爾西倉庫海灘宅  ·  Lovell Burton Architecture  ·  Anglesea, Victoria, Australia
Victorian Architecture Award – Houses (Alterations & Additions) · 2026
Design Reading
Lovell Burton Architecture converted a redundant 1970s industrial warehouse on the Anglesea waterfront into a permanent beach residence, receiving the Victorian Architecture Award for residential alterations and additions in 2026. The existing concrete masonry shell was retained wholesale; its raw interior volume is organised into day living, sleeping, and service zones beneath the exposed steel roof structure. Full-height bi-fold steel doors replace the former loading dock, opening the entire eastern elevation to the coastal reserve without threshold. Polished concrete floors extend to the terrace, raked plywood ceilings preserve the industrial character, and a salvaged-timber kitchen island provides textural counterpoint. The design makes no effort to conceal the building's industrial history—exposed roof trusses, raw masonry, and repurposed industrial hardware converted to lighting—allowing the warehouse character to become part of the beach residential experience. Photography by Rory Gardiner.

Eleanor

艾莉諾維多利亞改建宅  ·  Topology Studio  ·  Melbourne, Victoria, Australia
Victorian Architecture Award – Houses (Alterations & Additions) · 2026
Design Reading
Topology Studio inserted a series of domestic rooms within an unassuming Victorian worker's cottage in inner Melbourne, employing a "soft brutalism" of warm-toned pigmented concrete blockwork to define additions without competing with the existing brick, receiving the Victorian Architecture Award for residential alterations and additions in 2026. Ground-floor additions organise service functions in a linear block along the party wall, releasing the rear yard for a sunken garden terrace that mediates between the compressed Victorian room heights and the open Melbourne sky. A new upper-level bedroom volume is set back from the street ridge, preserving the heritage silhouette from the public realm. Polished concrete floors, handmade glazed tiles, and linen-toned joinery constitute a material register calibrated for warmth without sentiment. Photography by Tom Ross.

Mount Macedon House

麥其頓山林宅  ·  Ben Lance Architects  ·  Mount Macedon, Victoria, Australia
Commendation – Houses (New) · Victorian Architecture Awards 2026
Design Reading
Ben Lance Architects positioned this 260-square-metre retreat within the dense stringybark forest of Mount Macedon, anchoring the house to a natural rock shelf to minimise earthworks, receiving a commendation in the 2026 Victorian Architecture Awards residential new category and covered by ArchDaily. The floor plan traces the site contour as a folded line, allowing the structure to step with the terrain rather than cut through it. Externally, charred-timber (shou sugi ban) cladding recedes into the forest canopy while providing the dual performance of fire resistance and rot resistance critical in Mount Macedon's high-bushfire-risk zone—this was a performance imperative, not a stylistic choice. Internally, spotted gum floors and white plaster ceilings create a strong light-dark contrast, giving residents the deliberate spatial transition from the sheltered dark exterior to the warm inhabited interior. Window positions are precisely calibrated to the distant mountain views, resisting full glazing to maintain the sheltered quality of a forest dwelling rather than an observation deck. Photography by Josh Farmelo.

House Freddie

弗雷迪宅  ·  Studio Bright  ·  Melbourne, Victoria, Australia
Commendation – Houses (Alterations & Additions) · Victorian Architecture Awards 2026
Design Reading
Studio Bright delivered a compact 110-square-metre Melbourne extension and internal reconfiguration for a 1920s bungalow, centring the intervention on a new kitchen-dining volume defined by a curved concrete-block form that received a commendation in the 2026 Victorian Architecture Awards residential alterations and additions category. The curve provides spatial generosity within a geometrically constrained extension footprint while naturally directing views from the kitchen core toward the pocket garden. Raked ceilings follow the external roof pitch throughout the addition, maintaining heights above three metres and eliminating the compression typical of compact extensions. Cast concrete benchtops, handmade white-glazed ceramic tiles, and raw linen-toned joinery constitute a three-part material register: concrete contributes structural weight, handmade tile introduces textural warmth, linen timber softens the palette—three elements resolved within a deliberately low-contrast colour field. The kitchen's terminal view is set to the layered greens of the pocket garden, creating residential depth beyond the footprint. Photography by Gavin Green.
